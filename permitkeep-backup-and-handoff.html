A backup is only useful if the next person can restore it.

A renewal ledger needs a recovery plan, not just a download button. Use this checklist before changing devices, clearing browser data, or handing responsibility to another operator.

Restore replaces data. It does not merge two working ledgers.

Download a backup of the destination browser before importing anything. Rehearse in a separate browser profile first, not over the only current copy.

1. Choose the right export

  • Download backup: the JSON recovery file contains deadline records and any stored Pro work plans and local activity history. Use this for restore.
  • Export deadlines CSV: a readable deadline list for review or a spreadsheet. It is not a full-fidelity restore file.
  • Export calendar: a snapshot for a calendar application. It does not establish two-way synchronization or automatically update when you edit PermitKeep.

Export and restore are free. A data backup does not include the signed Pro license or activate Pro in another browser. PermitKeep Pro is not currently for sale; checkout remains disabled.

Before sharing an export, check what leaves your browser

Local processing does not make a downloaded file private after you send it. Search and dashboard filters are viewing tools, not export privacy controls: the backup and deadline CSV use the full ledger, and the calendar includes every record that has an expiration date.

  1. Choose the smallest useful handoff. A reviewer who only needs a few deadlines usually does not need your recovery backup. Prepare a separate, approved summary for that purpose. Keep the original JSON backup intact for recovery; do not delete working records or edit the backup to make a shareable subset.
  2. Inspect the actual file. Deadline CSV includes names, categories, locations, permit numbers, agencies, official links, owners, dates, lead windows, renewal-start dates, and notes. JSON also includes stored work plans and local history. The calendar contains renewal-start and expiration events with permit names, locations, agencies, and permit numbers; records without expiration dates are omitted. A short on-screen list does not mean a short export.
  3. Check the receiving service. Confirm the recipient and access permissions before uploading. A calendar import, shared folder, email attachment, or spreadsheet service has its own sharing and retention settings. Verify the destination calendar is not public or shared more widely than intended. PermitKeep does not control those services.
  4. Keep recovery and review copies distinct. Label a reduced or redacted summary as a review copy, with its date and scope. It is not a complete backup. Do not remove owners, notes, or work-plan data from the only recovery file just because a recipient should not receive them.
  5. If you shared too much, respond through your business process. Stop further distribution, restrict or revoke access where the receiving service permits it, notify the responsible person, and follow your organization's incident procedure. Deleting a local download or changing PermitKeep cannot recall copies already downloaded by someone else. Do not post the exposed file publicly while requesting help.

Final send check: correct recipient; minimum necessary information; actual file inspected; destination permissions checked; original recovery copy retained. Never send license codes, payment-return URLs, or sensitive filing contents with a routine ledger handoff.

2. Save a recovery copy outside the browser

  1. Open the working browser profile and check that you can see the expected locations and records. Clear search and select All before checking your inventory.
  2. In Portable by design, choose Download backup. Confirm that the JSON file actually arrived in your download folder.
  3. Keep a dated copy in a business-controlled backup location separate from the working browser. Follow your organization's access and retention policy; do not overwrite the last known-good copy.
  4. Record who exported it, the date, the record count, and the browser profile used. Export again after meaningful changes and before any device or browser migration.

The export is plain data, not an encrypted vault. Anyone with the file can read its contents. Do not add passwords, card details, tax IDs, identity documents, or sensitive filings to the tracker. If you choose cloud storage for your backup, its privacy and access controls apply separately.

3. Rehearse recovery without risking the original

  1. Create a separate browser profile and open the same trusted PermitKeep app URL. Leave the original profile untouched.
  2. If the destination already has data, download its backup first. Select Restore backup and choose the JSON file. Read any replacement confirmation before proceeding.
  3. Compare the restored record count, locations, owners, expiration dates, lead windows, and official links against the original. Spot-check urgent and missing-date records, not only the first row.
  4. If the source includes Pro data, keep it in the backup even when Pro is inactive in the destination. An inactive license is not evidence that work-plan data was deleted. Confirm Pro workflows separately with a valid development license where authorized.
  5. Download a new backup from the destination and compare the records and stored work-plan/history content. Export timestamps can differ. Keep the original recovery file until the handoff is accepted.

If the file is rejected or storage cannot be written, stop. Preserve the original file and working browser. Do not clear the source browser's data to troubleshoot the destination, and do not hand-edit a backup just to bypass validation.

4. Make the handoff explicit

Two browsers holding copies are not a shared team account. Agree which profile becomes the working ledger and who is responsible for updates. Otherwise two well-intentioned operators can create conflicting versions.

  • Outgoing owner: export a final backup and identify urgent renewals, missing information, agency contacts, and unfinished work.
  • Incoming owner: complete the restore check, verify priority dates with the issuing agencies, and accept responsibility for the next review.
  • Both: record the handoff date and backup filename through an approved business channel. Retain prior copies according to policy; avoid casual email attachments containing private operational details.

If the rehearsal fails, keep the source intact

  • Counts differ: clear search and filters in both profiles, confirm the selected backup's date, and compare the re-exported records. Do not approve a handoff with unexplained omissions.
  • Dates or ownership differ: confirm that you restored the intended final copy. Resolve conflicting edits with the responsible operator; restore is replacement, not conflict resolution.
  • Pro controls are unavailable: check data preservation separately from license activation. Keep the backup; do not delete work-plan fields to make the interface look complete.
  • Destination storage is unavailable: stop and retain the source and downloaded backup. Choose a usable destination profile and repeat the rehearsal before accepting it as the working ledger.

A successful import message is only the start of acceptance. The incoming owner should compare actual records and confirm the next operational review before the outgoing owner retires a working copy.

Two people edited different copies. Which one should you keep?

The newest filename is not necessarily the most complete ledger. A later export can still omit changes made in another browser. Do not import one copy over the other until an accountable owner has compared them.

  1. Preserve both versions. Ask each operator to download a fresh JSON backup and record its source profile and export date. Keep both originals unchanged in approved storage. Agree a short pause on ledger edits while reconciling; continue handling urgent renewals through your normal operational channels.
  2. Compare in separate profiles. Restore each backup into its own rehearsal profile, never the only working profile. Compare locations, permit names, owners, deadlines, official links, and unfinished steps. If Pro data is present, include stored work-plan and history content in the comparison; do not treat unavailable Pro controls as missing data.
  3. Resolve facts, not just timestamps. For each difference, record the two values, the responsible owner, the agency evidence needed, and the decision. An export date or local activity entry is not proof that a deadline or renewal status is correct. Keep unresolved facts explicitly pending.
  4. Rebuild one accepted working copy. Select a baseline only after review. Re-enter agreed corrections through the app rather than editing JSON to evade validation. PermitKeep does not automatically merge backups or combine the separate local histories; retain both originals as context.
  5. Accept and retire deliberately. Download the reconciled backup, rehearse its restore, and compare the agreed corrections. Name one working profile and owner, tell other operators to stop editing retired copies, and schedule the next backup. Retain superseded copies according to your organization's policy.

Minimal reconciliation note: record/location reference; value in copy A; value in copy B; evidence checked; agreed value or pending question; decision owner; next check date. Keep this note in an approved business record, not a public support post. This is a manual recovery method, not a shared-account or automatic merge feature.

Changing browser or app address? Treat it as a data migration.

An installed app window, a browser tab, and a shortcut can look alike while opening different profiles or website addresses. An empty dashboard at a new address does not prove that your old records were deleted. Do not reset either profile just to make the screens match.

  1. Identify the working source. Reopen the browser profile and trusted app address you used before. Check the full address, including the hostname, and clear search and filters. If the old profile still shows records, leave it intact and download a dated JSON backup.
  2. Verify the destination independently. Use the maker's known product page or an already trusted bookmark to confirm a new address. Do not paste a backup or license into a lookalike site, unsolicited support form, or search advertisement. Stop if you cannot establish which site is genuine.
  3. Restore a copy, not your only copy. Follow the rehearsal above in a separate destination profile. Back up any existing destination records first; restore replaces rather than merges. Keep the original file and source profile available.
  4. Check persistence before switching. Compare records and stored Pro data, then close and reopen the destination app and compare again. Download a destination backup. A successful import does not establish that private browsing, managed-device policy, or later site-data cleanup will retain records.
  5. Update shortcuts only after acceptance. Record the accepted app address and browser profile, update your bookmark, and name the operator responsible for the next backup. Retire old working copies deliberately; do not clear source data while a mismatch remains unresolved.

If the original profile is gone and no backup exists, changing addresses or reinstalling the app cannot reconstruct the ledger. Rebuild from your approved business records and verify deadlines with the issuing agencies. PermitKeep support cannot retrieve a hosted copy of your records.

Safe support details: describe the browser, whether you changed profile or app address, and the visible error wording. Redact private record details. Never post JSON backups, license codes, payment-return URLs, or screenshots containing operational or payment information in a public issue.

A save failed. Preserve the draft before trying again.

A visible edit is not proof that the browser stored it. If saving fails, stop making further changes until you have protected both the last saved ledger and the unsaved information. Do not clear site data, reset the app, reinstall it, or repeatedly press Save as a first response.

  1. Keep the current window open. Before reloading or closing it, copy the unsaved changes into an approved private business record: the permit reference, field names, intended values, and any unfinished step details. Label this as an unsaved draft, not an accepted renewal record. Do not paste it into a public support issue.
  2. Preserve a saved-state backup if export is still available. Keep it under a new dated filename without replacing the last known-good backup. A backup of saved records does not necessarily include what you just typed into an unfinished form. Keep the draft note separately.
  3. Check for competing working copies. Ask other operators to pause edits and inspect other open tabs or app windows in the same profile. Preserve their unsaved drafts before closing them. Choose one working window; separate tabs are not a collaboration or automatic merge system.
  4. Reopen only after preserving the evidence. Confirm the trusted app address and browser profile, then reload the chosen working window. Compare the saved record with your draft before re-entering anything. If another operator changed the record, reconcile the facts with that person rather than overwriting their work.
  5. Verify the recovery, not just the message. Save the agreed change, then reopen the app and check the exact record and values again. Export a fresh backup and retain prior recovery copies under your policy. If saving still fails, leave the source profile intact and use the separate-profile restore rehearsal above; do not clear the only source to make room.

If a blocking recovery screen appears: preserve any raw-data download it offers and the original backup. A diagnostic or raw-data file is not automatically a valid restore backup. Do not edit it to bypass validation or reset the source until you have a verified recovery copy. Continue urgent renewal work through your normal business process while the ledger is unavailable.

This procedure reduces avoidable loss; it is not a guarantee of atomic saves, conflict-free simultaneous edits, or browser retention. Support cannot reconstruct missing unsaved changes from a hosted record database.

Internet access is down. Protect the ledger and keep urgent work moving.

Offline access depends on this browser profile having successfully loaded and cached the app beforehand. A bookmark or installed shortcut alone is not proof that an offline copy is ready. A different device or new profile cannot download the app for the first time without a connection.

  1. Use the known working profile. Open the trusted app address in the profile you normally use. If it opens, check your records and download a dated backup before making further changes. Do not clear site data, uninstall the app, or switch profiles as a first troubleshooting step: that can remove or separate you from the available local copy.
  2. Separate local work from external verification. You may be able to review and edit locally stored records while offline, but agency websites, filing portals, payments, and external calendar services still depend on their own availability. A working dashboard does not establish that an agency received a filing or that a renewal is complete.
  3. Keep an outage note if the app will not open. Use your approved private business records to capture the permit reference, intended change, responsible person, evidence still needed, and next follow-up. Label it as pending reconciliation. Use previously exported records only as dated snapshots; do not assume they include later changes. Follow your normal urgent-renewal escalation process rather than waiting for the tracker.
  4. Reconnect without overwriting newer work. Return to the same trusted profile, preserve any unsaved drafts, and back up the currently visible ledger. Compare each outage note with the actual record and any other operator's changes before entering it. Verify agency facts separately. Do not restore an old backup over newer records just because the old file opens.
  5. Check the accepted copy. Reopen the app after saving, compare the exact changed values, and export a fresh backup. Record unresolved discrepancies and their owner. Retain the original backup and outage notes until reconciliation is accepted under your organization's policy.

Prepare before an outage: after protecting the working ledger and finishing unsaved edits, check whether the trusted app reopens without a network connection in your usual profile. Restore connectivity afterward. Keep a separately accessible backup and the contact details needed for urgent agency follow-up. One successful check is not a guarantee against later cache eviction, browser cleanup, or device failure.

This is a manual continuity procedure, not an offline filing service, automatic synchronization, or a promise that all guides remain available offline. Downloaded backups are plain data; protect them even when normal online tools are unavailable.

5. Know what a local app cannot recover

Browser storage belongs to a particular browser profile and website origin. Another device, profile, or app address will not automatically see the same records. Clearing site data, losing the device, or deleting the profile can remove the working ledger. PermitKeep has no hosted record database from which support can retrieve a missing backup.

Calendar reminders depend on your calendar's own import and notification settings. Reimporting snapshots may create duplicates; check your calendar before relying on it. Neither a backup nor a calendar entry confirms that a permit was renewed or that a filing was accepted.

Verify legal and agency requirements separately.

This is a data-handling workflow, not a retention schedule, filing service, or compliance guarantee. Confirm required records, dates, filing status, and retention duties with the agency and qualified advisers.
