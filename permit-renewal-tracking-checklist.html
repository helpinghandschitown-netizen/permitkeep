A permit renewal checklist that survives busy weeks

The hard part of renewal tracking is rarely making a list. It is keeping the dates, owner, official source, and next action visible across locations—without assuming last year’s process is still correct.

1. Build one inventory

List every business license, operating permit, inspection, certification, registration, and other recurring approval you already know about. Use one row or record per item and location. Do not combine several locations just because they share a name; dates and issuing agencies can differ.

  • Plain-language name and category
  • Exact location it applies to
  • Issuing agency and official agency page
  • Permit or account number, if safe to store
  • Expiration date and the date renewal work should begin
  • One accountable person or role

2. Verify from an official source

Use the issuing agency—not a search snippet, old email, vendor blog, or last year’s calendar—as the source of truth. Confirm the current date, fee, form, filing method, and required documents. Record the official link and the date you checked it. If a requirement is unclear, contact the agency or a qualified local adviser.

3. Set a realistic lead window

Work backward from expiration. Include time to gather documents, obtain signatures, complete inspections, correct rejected material, and account for agency processing. A reminder on the expiration date is not a renewal plan. Use a separate renewal-start date that gives the owner time to act.

4. Assign one owner and one handoff

“Office” or “management” is often too vague. Name a person or a stable role, then decide who takes over during leave or turnover. Keep the owner visible beside the deadline instead of burying responsibility in notes.

5. Review by exception

At a regular operations check, look first for expired items, records inside their renewal window, and records missing dates. Then review upcoming items. This keeps urgent exceptions visible without forcing the team to scan every current record.

6. Back up and export

A browser profile, spreadsheet, or device can fail. Keep a current backup somewhere your business controls and periodically test that it restores. Export calendar events for day-to-day visibility, but keep the full ledger as the authoritative working list.

7. Check the calendar handoff before relying on it

A calendar export is a snapshot, not a subscription. Changing a record in PermitKeep does not update an already imported calendar. Use the ledger for current facts and the calendar as a secondary view.

  1. Check what is included. Each record with an expiration date creates two all-day events: “Start renewal” and “Expires.” Records without dates are omitted. Work-plan steps and local activity history are not included in this calendar export; review those separately.
  2. Choose a private destination. Event titles contain the record name; descriptions can contain the location, agency, and permit number. Review calendar sharing permissions before import. A local export does not make the calendar provider private or prevent invited viewers from seeing its contents.
  3. Try a separate calendar first. If your calendar supports it, create a dedicated PermitKeep calendar rather than mixing an initial import with unrelated appointments. Check one renewal-start date and one expiration date against the ledger, including month and year. All-day events do not specify an agency’s filing cutoff time.
  4. Set and test notifications yourself. The exported file contains no alarm instructions. Whether an import produces a notification depends on your calendar’s settings. Configure the alerts you need and test delivery on the device you actually use. PermitKeep does not send email or SMS reminders.
  5. Reconcile after a date changes. Back up the ledger, export a fresh file, and check how your calendar handles re-imports. Do not assume it replaces earlier events: remove or update obsolete imported entries deliberately, without deleting unrelated appointments. Deleted ledger records do not automatically disappear from a calendar.
  6. Keep missing dates on the review list. An empty calendar is not proof that nothing is due. Review undated records in the ledger, name an owner to confirm each date, and use the weekly renewal worksheet to track follow-up.

Close the handoff: record who checked the import, when it was exported, which calendar received it, and who will reconcile changes. A calendar file is not a full backup; keep the JSON backup for restoring the working ledger. See the backup and handoff guide before changing devices or browser profiles.

This checklist does not determine what your business requires.

Permit and license rules vary and change. Confirm all requirements, dates, fees, filing status, and retention duties with the issuing agency and qualified local professionals. Never place passwords, payment-card data, tax IDs, identity documents, or other secrets in a deadline tracker.
