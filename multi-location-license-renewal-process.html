Multi-location operations guide

One renewal process, without pretending every location is the same

A shared business name does not create shared deadlines. Each location can have a different issuing office, account number, inspection sequence, expiration date, or renewal lead time. The safest operating pattern is one record per approval per location, with a visible owner and official source.

Separate the records before you sort them

Do not keep “all store licenses” in one row. Split the inventory into distinct records such as “Downtown food establishment license” and “Northside food establishment license.” If one approval covers several locations, say that explicitly and retain the issuing agency’s evidence of the coverage.

  • Use the location name employees actually recognize.
  • Preserve the agency’s exact permit or account number where it is appropriate to store.
  • Attach the official agency page, not a search result or third-party summary.
  • Record the expiration date separately from the date renewal work should begin.

Give each renewal a readiness window

Choose a lead window based on the work required, not a universal reminder interval. A simple registration may need only a short preparation period. An approval involving inspections, insurance certificates, signatures, or several departments may need much longer. Confirm the process with the issuing agency and revise the lead window when the real workflow changes.

Assign an owner and a backup owner

One person or stable role should own the next action. Keep the backup owner in your operating procedure or notes so a vacation or departure does not make the deadline invisible. A calendar invite can support the handoff, but it should not be the only record of the renewal.

Run a short exception review

At each operations review, examine records in this order:

  1. Expired records and anything whose status is uncertain.
  2. Records inside the renewal-start window.
  3. Records missing an expiration date or official source.
  4. Upcoming records whose owner or process changed.

The review should end with a concrete next action, owner, and date. “Watching it” is not a handoff.

Reconcile location reports before combining them

Choose one person to maintain the current master ledger. Ask each location owner to confirm their own records against agency evidence, then have the ledger owner review and enter the changes. PermitKeep does not synchronize browsers or merge competing backups. A copied backup is a snapshot, not a shared workspace; confirm which copy is current before anyone restores it.

Match records by location and the specific approval, not the display name alone. If two reports disagree, retain the last confirmed fact and record the uncertainty separately until the owner checks the issuing authority. Do not replace an unknown date with an estimated expiration or mark a renewal complete merely because an application was submitted.

A snapshot is not synchronization. Keep one identified master; do not restore a competing copy over newer work. Submission alone does not establish renewal. Keep passwords, tax IDs and sensitive filings off this worksheet.

Keep evidence outside the deadline ledger

A tracker should point to the work, not become a vault for sensitive filings. Keep submitted forms, identity documents, payment receipts, passwords, tax identifiers, and regulated records in an appropriately secured system. PermitKeep is designed for operational dates and context, and its browser storage is not encrypted by the app.

Plan for browser and staff turnover

Export a PermitKeep backup on a regular schedule and after important changes. Store it somewhere the business controls, restrict access, and test a restore before an emergency. Export calendar events for day-to-day visibility and CSV for review, but keep the JSON backup as the portable record set that PermitKeep can restore.

Confirm every requirement with the issuing authority.

This process organizes facts your business enters. It does not identify required permits, interpret rules, monitor agencies, file renewals, or guarantee that an approval remains valid. Requirements vary by activity and location and can change.
