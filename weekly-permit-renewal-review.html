Run a weekly renewal review that ends with named next actions.

A list of dates is a starting point. Use this short meeting agenda and blank worksheet to decide what needs attention, who will act, and what evidence closes the loop.

Print this page or save it as PDF using your browser's Print command. Fill the worksheet on paper or in your own approved document tool. This page has no form submission and does not save answers.

Submitted does not mean renewed.

A sent application, payment receipt, or completed internal task is not proof that an agency approved a renewal. Confirm status and effective dates with the issuing authority before changing your working deadline.

Before the meeting

  • Open your working ledger, clear filters, and check that every active location is represented.
  • Have the agency's current instructions and your authorized filing records available separately. Do not paste sensitive filings or credentials into the tracker.
  • Bring unresolved items from the last review. If a date or requirement is uncertain, mark it unverified instead of inventing one.

A repeatable review order

  1. Expired or uncertain: identify the owner who will contact the agency. Do not assume continued operation is permitted while a renewal is pending; seek agency guidance and qualified advice.
  2. Renewal work due now: compare the entered lead window with the actual next steps. Assign one owner and one target date for each blocker.
  3. Upcoming: check dependencies such as inspections, signatures, or supporting records against current official instructions. No generic lead time is safe for every permit.
  4. Awaiting a response: record the official status, when it was checked, and the next follow-up action. Keep the original deadline visible until authoritative evidence supports an update.
  5. Close the review: read back assignments, agree the next review date, and save a new backup after updating the ledger.

An example of a useful action

Fictional example: “Operations lead: ask the issuing agency to confirm whether the submitted renewal is complete; record the response by the team's agreed follow-up date.” This is more actionable than “handle permit.” The follow-up date is an internal target, not an agency deadline or permission to keep operating.

Keep the worksheet and the working ledger distinct

The worksheet records review decisions. Transfer verified changes to your chosen working ledger so that the paper sheet does not become a conflicting second database. Store and dispose of printed copies under your business's access and retention policies.
